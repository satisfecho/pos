Manual for customers and restaurant teams

How to use Satisfecho POS from start to finish

This guide explains the full application with practical steps: initial setup, tables, orders, payments, reservations, kitchen, bar, inventory, reports, shifts, contracts, feedback, and the supplier portal. Screenshots are real captures from the demo environment.

Updated: May 2026 Web application Roles: owner, admin, staff, and supplier Includes public and private screens

Read this first

Satisfecho POS is split into three experiences. Staff work from the internal dashboard; customers use the public menu from a QR code, link, or table search; and suppliers use a separate portal when the restaurant wants to receive external product catalogs.

If you are new, start with Settings, then create your products, set up tables and QR, and finally test the full flow for the customer order and staff operations.

Practical rule

Configure the restaurant first. Then load products, tables, staff, and payment methods. Do not publish QR codes or customer links until you have run a real test of order, kitchen, payment, and table close.

1. Getting started

Public home screen

The home page shows available restaurants, links to book, log in, use a table by code, and, if configured, place takeaway orders. It also includes supplier access and the language selector.

Create the first account

Sign-up creates a restaurant account. The first person who registers a restaurant should be the one who will manage the initial setup, because that account is used to create users, products, tables, payments, and settings.

  1. Go to Sign up.
  2. Enter name, email, password, and basic restaurant details.
  3. Save. Then log in with the email and password you just created.
  4. Complete settings before you share public links.

Log in and recover password

Staff enter through Log in. If the restaurant enabled two-step verification, the system asks for a temporary code after the password. If someone forgets their password, they must use the recovery link and follow the email they receive.

Internal dashboard and navigation

After you log in, the internal dashboard appears. The side menu shows only the sections allowed by the role and by the modules enabled in settings. On small screens it works as a drop-down menu.

Languages. The application lets you change language from the public area and from the internal environment. If a label appears in another language, it usually means that translation is not yet loaded for that part of the system.

2. Roles and permissions

Permissions protect sensitive actions. If a user does not see a section, it is not an error: their role lacks permission or the module was turned off in Settings > Navigation.

Role Recommended use Main permissions Important limits
Owner Owner or top responsible person. Full access: settings, users, products, tables, reservations, reports, inventory, contracts, and data. This must be a protected account. Sharing it is not recommended.
Admin Operations manager. Manages daily operations, non-owner users, products, tables, reservations, inventory, reports, contracts, and the working plan. May have restrictions on billing data, payment keys, or owner users.
Waiter Floor service. Sees products, tables, reservations, fiscal customers, and orders. Can update statuses, mark paid, and remove allowed lines. Does not manage settings, users, inventory, or global reports.
Receptionist Reservations and first contact. Manages reservations, and can view tables, customers, and orders. Does not change products or settings; order access is more limited.
Kitchen Food preparation. Sees orders and changes statuses of lines assigned to kitchen. Also sees their shift and contracts. Does not take payment or manage tables.
Bar Drink preparation. Similar to kitchen, focused on the bar screen and drink products. Does not take payment or manage settings.
Supplier External supplier. Uses a separate portal for their catalog and company data. Does not enter the restaurant internal dashboard.
Best practices. Disable users who no longer work with you and avoid shared accounts. For sensitive changes, use owner or admin with an individual password.

3. Settings

Settings control how the restaurant looks and behaves. Do this part carefully before you go live: it affects QR codes, reservations, payments, hours, security, kitchen, inventory, and module visibility.

Profile, branding, and public appearance

In Profile you edit the restaurant name, description, phone, email, currency, country, time zone, logo, header image, and public background color. These details appear on public pages, reservations, the QR menu, and some messages.

Navigation and modules

The Navigation tab lets you enable or disable modules such as tables, working plan, suppliers, reservations, kitchen/bar, and inventory. If a module is off, the menu hides it and direct links may redirect to the internal home.

  • Turn off modules your restaurant does not use to simplify staff work.
  • Enable inventory only when you will maintain stock, suppliers, purchases, and recipes.
  • Enable kitchen/bar if you need preparation screens by station.

Contact, location, legal, and reviews

In Contact you store address, website, Google Maps and OpenStreetMap links, Google reviews URL, tax ID (NIF/CIF), bank account (CCC), public terms URL, public privacy URL, and basic fiscal data. You also set the default tax when a product has no specific rule.

Hours, expected shifts, and exceptions

In Hours you set the restaurant weekly schedule, staffing needs by time slot, and patterns planned for the future. You can also create exceptions for specific dates, for example holidays, vacations, or private events.

Payments, invoicing, tips, and location verification

In Payments you adjust visible payment methods, tips, payment behavior, and payment provider details such as Stripe or Revolut when they are enabled in your installation. There may also be options for fiscal invoices, internal series, test or production mode, and location verification for in-person payments.

Tax compliance. Fiscal settings depend on the country and the deployment. Before you use fiscal invoicing in production, confirm with your advisor and the technical team which integration is active.

Public reservations

Reservation settings control whether customers can book, whether prepayment is required, the cancellation policy, arrival tolerance, average table duration, slot interval, maximum people per slot, tables for walk-in guests, dress code, and 24-hour or 2-hour reminders.

  • Use reservation intervals your team can meet, for example 15 or 30 minutes.
  • Set arrival tolerance so you know when to mark no-show.
  • Enable reminders only when email or messaging is correctly configured.

Outgoing email

If your deployment has SMTP enabled, use it for password recovery, reminders, confirmations, and reservation-related messages. Test email before you enable automatic reminders.

Taxes and VAT

In Taxes you can create VAT types or equivalent taxes. Then each product can use the restaurant default tax or its own rule.

Kitchen stations

Stations group preparation work. A product can be assigned to a station so it appears where it belongs: hot kitchen, desserts, bar, grill, or another area.

Delivery integrations

In Integrations you configure marketplaces or external channels when the deployment includes the matching adapter. Keep technical links and secrets protected.

Social posts

Social posts let you prepare messages and content for connected channels, such as Meta, when the OAuth connection is configured. Use it for promotions, news, or seasonal campaigns.

Security, clock-in, and data

In Security you can enable two-step codes for sensitive access and configure staff clock-in with QR. If location verification is enabled, the device will ask for location permission when clocking in.

In Data and privacy, the owner can export restaurant information and, if the deployment allows it, run destructive actions such as purging data. These actions must stay with the owner and require a prior backup.

Templates, translations, and internal suppliers

Settings also include contract templates, translations of visible texts, and the restaurant’s own suppliers/products. These areas help keep documents, public labels, and purchases aligned with the same base information.

4. Products and catalog

Products is the heart of the menu. Each product can have price, cost, image, category, subcategory, ingredients, description, its own tax, kitchen station, availability by time, and customization questions.

Create or edit a product

  1. Go to Products and open the products tab.
  2. Enter name, sale price, and, if you know it, purchase or prep cost.
  3. Add description and ingredients. This helps customers and staff with allergies.
  4. Select category, subcategory, and kitchen station when it applies.
  5. Upload a real, clear image of the dish or drink.
  6. If the product is sold only at certain times, set availability from/to.
  7. Save. Then add customization questions if the product needs them.

Questions and customizations

After you save a product you can add questions for the customer or waiter: single choice, multiple choice, scale, or free text. Each question can be required and have an order. Use them for cooking preference, sizes, sauces, extras, removals, or notes.

Example. For a burger, create a required question "Cooking level" with options rare, medium, and well done. Create another for extras if they have a cost.

Costs, recipes, and ideal price

If inventory and recipes are active, you can link real ingredients to the product. The system uses those costs to help calculate margin and ideal price. This help works well only if stock, units, and costs are up to date.

Supplier catalog

The Catalog section gathers supplier products or external products that the restaurant reviews for purchases, availability, or reference. It does not replace the customer sales menu: the menu customers see comes from Products.

5. Tables and floor plan

Tables connect the floor to orders. Each table can have a name, capacity, status, floor, QR, PIN, and assigned waiter. You can also work with list, card, or visual floor-plan views.

What to configure on each table

  • Visible name: for example T01, Terrace 4, or Bar 2.
  • Capacity: normal number of guests.
  • Floor or zone: dining room, terrace, bar, private, or another area.
  • PIN: protects customer actions such as confirming or changing orders.
  • Assigned waiter: can be set by floor and adjusted per table.
  • Active: only active tables are used in operations and public links.

QR, link, and staff-opened menu

The table QR opens that table’s public menu. You can copy the link to print it or send it. The Open menu action lets staff help a table from their device; it must not be used as a permanent public link.

Table groups and reassignment

You can join tables when several tables form one check or a large reservation. If you try to delete a table with linked orders or reservations, the system may ask you to reassign information so history is not lost.

Visual floor plan

The floor plan helps you quickly see which tables are free, occupied, with payment pending, assigned to a waiter, or grouped. Colors and payment chips help you spot pending actions without opening each table.

6. Customer experience

Customers can use the menu without installing anything. They enter via table QR, link, table search on the public page, or the takeaway order path when the restaurant has it active.

Initial identification

When entering the table menu, the customer can enter their name. This helps staff recognize who placed the order and resolve questions at the table.

Browse products

The menu shows categories, subcategories, featured items, images, prices, ingredients, allergens, or dietary details when they are loaded. The customer adds products with the plus button.

Cart, customization, and confirmation

Before sending, the customer reviews quantities, customizations, and total. If the product has required questions, they must answer them before confirming. To avoid accidental orders, the table can require a PIN.

Active order, history, and actions

After sending, the customer sees the order status. They can keep adding products if the table is still open. Depending on settings and status, they can request payment, call the waiter, cancel items that are still allowed, or view history.

Payment options visible to the customer

Options depend on restaurant settings. Online payment, terminal, cash, Revolut, Stripe, or call waiter may appear. If online payment is not configured, the customer can ask staff to take payment at the table.

Takeaway or delivery orders

If the restaurant has a takeaway table or channel configured, the public orders page shows available restaurants. The flow is similar to the table menu, but it is used for orders without sitting at a physical table.

7. Daily operations

The orders section is the center of service. Staff can see active orders, unpaid checks, and history. There are also separate screens for kitchen and bar.

Active orders

In Orders you see tables, lines, statuses, notes, and totals. From here you can add products, edit orders, remove allowed lines, mark urgent, advance statuses, and open payment options.

Order and line statuses

An order can move through statuses such as received, preparing, ready, delivered, paid, or cancelled. Each line can also have its own status, so kitchen and bar can work in parallel.

  • Received: the customer or staff just sent the order.
  • Preparing: kitchen or bar is working on it.
  • Ready: it is prepared for delivery.
  • Delivered: it has reached the table.
  • Paid: the check is closed.

Payment, tip, and invoice

In the payment modal you can choose the method, record a tip, manage overpayment when it applies, finalize the order, and open an invoice. Visible methods depend on settings: cash, terminal, Stripe, Revolut, or others.

History

History lets you review previous orders, amounts, statuses, and removed items when that option is visible. It is useful for resolving questions, internal audit, and reports.

Kitchen screen

Kitchen shows the lines that need preparation. The team can advance statuses without entering payments or settings. If you use stations, each product goes to the assigned area.

Bar screen

Bar works as a preparation screen focused on drinks or a similar station. It is especially useful when the bar must work separately from the kitchen.

8. Reservations and feedback

Internal reservations

In Reservations staff can create, search, and manage bookings. The view lets you filter by date, phone, or status, see possible overbooking, send reminders, assign a table, mark arrival, mark no-show, cancel, or complete.

Public booking

Customers can book from the restaurant public page. The form shows date, shift, available time, number of guests, zone or floor preference when it is enabled, contact details, allergies, and notes.

Manage a reservation from the customer link

When the customer receives a reservation link, they can view details, cancel if the policy allows it, or report a delay. This reduces calls and keeps the team informed.

Customer feedback

The system includes a public feedback page and an internal inbox. You can share the link or QR after the visit. In the internal inbox you review ratings, comments, and related data.

9. Team, shifts, and contracts

Users

In Users you create staff accounts, assign roles, and update details. Use real emails and avoid sharing credentials. The role determines real access, not only the visible menu.

My shift

My shift lets the worker see their shift status, clock in or out if enabled, view breaks, and review information related to their day.

Weekly working plan and calendar

The working plan lets you organize shifts by week and review a calendar. It is fed by restaurant hours, staffing needs, and contracts. It helps you see coverage, assign staff, and spot gaps before service.

Contracts

Contracts centralizes staff contracts and templates. Depending on permissions, the user can read, create, update, or manage documents. Use it with the working plan so hours, duties, and conditions stay aligned.

10. Inventory, customers, and reports

Fiscal customers

The Customers section stores data needed for invoices or fiscal identification. Authorized staff can create and edit customers to link them with orders and invoices.

Inventory: items and stock

Inventory lets you control items, SKUs, categories, units, cost, minimum stock, default supplier, and movements. Stock adjustments can record receipts, issues, waste, or corrections.

Suppliers and purchase orders

You can create suppliers, generate purchase orders, change statuses, print PDF, and receive goods to update stock. When receiving, check quantities and prices before you confirm.

Inventory reports

Inventory reports help detect low stock, consumption, cost, and purchases. They are more useful when recipes and movements are recorded with discipline.

Sales and operations reports

Reports shows revenue, tips, costs, profit, average payment, average per customer, daily sales, breakdowns, and exports when available. Use it to make decisions on menu, staff, and hours.

11. Supplier portal

The supplier portal is separate from the internal dashboard. A supplier can register, log in, recover password, edit company details, and maintain their catalog with images, prices, and availability. Currently some portal screens may appear in English.

  • The supplier does not manage floor orders or restaurant settings.
  • The restaurant decides whether to use supplier products as reference or catalog.
  • Bank, fiscal, and contact data must be reviewed by the supplier.
When to use it. Use it if you work with suppliers who keep live product lists. If you always buy manually, internal inventory may be enough.

12. Checklists

Initial go-live

  • Create the owner account and store credentials securely.
  • Complete profile, contact, location, taxes, and time zone.
  • Configure hours, reservations, and arrival tolerance.
  • Enable only the needed modules in navigation.
  • Create products, categories, images, and required questions.
  • Create floors, tables, PIN, and QR.
  • Create real users with the correct roles.
  • Test a full order: customer, kitchen/bar, payment, and close.
  • Test a public reservation and its internal management.
  • Review reports and permissions before opening to the public.

Before each service

  • Verify that tables and QR codes are active.
  • Check sold-out or unavailable products.
  • Review today’s reservations and assigned tables.
  • Confirm that kitchen and bar can see their screens.
  • Review shift users and clock-ins when applicable.
  • Confirm payment methods and cash drawer.
  • Review low stock if inventory is active.

End of day

  • Close active orders and review unpaid checks.
  • Resolve cancelled orders or removed lines.
  • Review tips, payment methods, and invoices.
  • Record relevant waste or stock adjustments.
  • Look at the day’s reports and note incidents.
  • Review new feedback and respond internally.

Operational security

  • Do not share owner or admin accounts.
  • Do not send payment keys, SMTP, or tokens by chat.
  • Disable users who no longer work at the restaurant.
  • Use the minimum roles needed for each position.
  • Test settings changes before real service.
  • Export data before destructive actions.

13. Common problems

A user cannot see a section

Check two things: their role in Users and whether the module is active in Settings > Navigation. If both are correct, log out and log in again.

The customer cannot order from a table

Confirm that the table is active, that the QR matches that table, that the product is available at that time, and that the PIN given is correct. If the menu does not load products, review categories and availability.

A reservation slot is not available

Review restaurant hours, reservation rules, average table duration, slot interval, capacity, preferred zone, and existing reservations in the same slot.

Online payment does not appear

Online payment only appears when the method is active and correctly configured. If there is no payment provider, use cash, terminal, or call waiter.

Stock does not match

Review units, recipes, received purchases, and manual adjustments. A sold product only deducts stock correctly when the recipe and units are configured well.

Short glossary

Concept Meaning
Slot Available time block for booking.
Table PIN Code that confirms customer actions at a table.
Station Preparation area such as kitchen, bar, desserts, or grill.
Waste Lost, discarded, or unsellable product that must be deducted from stock.
No-show Confirmed reservation whose guest did not arrive within the defined tolerance.
